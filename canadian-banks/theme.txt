## VERDICT

Day three after the Fed's first hike since 2023 was a quiet one for Canadian banks: on 2026-09-18 ZEB returned +0.43% to C$75.28 on 1.17x its 20-day average volume while the TSX slipped -0.19% as industrials and energy weighed (MT Newswires TSX Closer, 2026-09-18); neither event threshold was met, so no new event was added. The week closed with banks modestly in front — ZEB +0.94% versus TSX +0.31% — as Wednesday's hawkish shock gave way to a Thursday reclaim and a flat-to-higher Friday. Over the refreshed 3-month window (now starting 2026-06-18) ZEB returned +2.01% versus the TSX's +2.39% — an excess of -0.39pp, effectively flat — while the S&P 500's +2.38% covers a window starting 2026-06-22 (two trading days later; all figures in local currencies, so FX moves are embedded). The relative-performance problem persists but narrowed: the 42-session excess improved to -2.70pp from -4.07pp, largely because July 20's -2.27% drawdown rolled out of the measurement window, so VALIDITY stays red on arithmetic rather than fresh weakness. In the US, the first full week after the hike ended with the 10-year Treasury yield trading near a 19-year high and weekly losses for the Dow and S&P 500 (MT Newswires, 2026-09-18); the policy backdrop is otherwise unchanged — the Fed raised its policy rate 25bp on September 16 in a unanimous decision and signaled one more hike in 2026, while the BoC held at 2.25% on September 2. The widening US-Canada policy gap feeds the currency and funding-cost channels, but pass-through to bank earnings remains a hypothesis, not an established effect.
